[gloom] 1997. The ocean made a sound so loud that scientists wondered what could make it.
[gloom] A US research agency, NOAA, was listening with underwater microphones, mainly for volcanoes.
[gloom] It picked up an ultra-low rumble, rising in pitch over about a minute. It was loud enough to be heard on sensors thousands of kilometres apart. They traced it to the South Pacific.
[gloom] It was named "the Bloop". The profile looked a little like an animal's call. But far too powerful for any animal on Earth. Internet theories: a giant creature, a secret test, a submarine.
[gloom] NOAA's researchers ruled out a man-made source early. One suspected ice breaking off Antarctica.
[gloom] Microphones moved closer to Antarctica. In 2005, the source was found. An icequake, an iceberg cracking and breaking away.
[gloom] Similar sounds were later recorded from icebergs breaking up near South Georgia.
[gloom] No monster. Just ice. Gloom: "A bit disappointing. Mostly."
