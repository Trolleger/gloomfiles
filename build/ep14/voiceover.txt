[gloom] Rocks in Death Valley move on their own. For decades, nobody saw it happen.
[gloom] Racetrack Playa is a flat, dry lake bed in California. Rocks sit on it with long trails behind them. The first scientific reports date from 1948.
[moth] Who's pushing them?
[gloom] Theories: dust devils, gale winds, slippery algae, thick sheets of ice. Nobody had a recording. Trails showed up, but the rocks never moved while anyone watched.
[gloom] In 2011, researchers put GPS trackers in 15 rocks, and added a weather station and cameras. One researcher expected "the most boring experiment ever".
[gloom] After a rare winter storm, a shallow pond formed. On December 20, 2013, more than 60 rocks moved.
[gloom] The answer: thin ice, 3 to 6 millimetres thick. At sunrise it breaks into panels. A light wind pushes the panels, and the panels push the rocks, a few metres a minute.
[gloom] No ghosts. A gentle breeze and some ice. Gloom: "Very disappointing. Very satisfying."
