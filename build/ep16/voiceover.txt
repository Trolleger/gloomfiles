[gloom] A hole in Nova Scotia. People have been digging it for over 230 years.
[gloom] Oak Island sits in Mahone Bay. The story starts in 1795, when a teenager reportedly found a dip in the ground near a large oak tree.
[gloom] Early accounts describe a shaft with oak-log platforms every ten feet. These come from accounts written decades later. Not documents.
[gloom] In 1804, diggers reached about 30 metres. The shaft flooded with sea water. Later digs found tunnels linking the pit to the ocean.
[gloom] One account says a stone found deep in the pit read: "Ten feet below two million lies buried." The stone is lost. The reading is one decoder's.
[gloom] A 1971 camera showed faint images that some thought were chests. Unverified.
[gloom] Skeptics point to the island's limestone, which can form sinkholes that fill with silt and wood.
[gloom] After more than 230 years: no treasure confirmed. No one has stopped looking.
