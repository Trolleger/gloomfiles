# Gloom Files: script rules (source of truth)

## Format
- About 60 s of narration (~150-170 words at the Piper en_US-joe-medium pace, ~2.9 words/s).
- Each episode is standalone. No cliffhangers.
- Open with a strong hook: the most striking claim or image in the first sentence.
- Scripts are written in batches and Andrew approves them before anything is built.

## Mine the source fully before writing
- Read the whole source (every post and reply, linked pages, the archive) before drafting. Write a source digest: every distinct claim, quote, odd detail, number, turn and reply, in order.
- The script is built from the most specific, surprising details in that digest: exact words people used, strange specifics, what made this thread different from any other. Paraphrase sharply rather than going generic.
- Generic summary is slop. If a line could describe any creepy thread ("the replies got stranger", "people started asking"), replace it with the concrete detail that actually happened.
- Keep details the source leans on (e.g. a title referencing Death Note) unless Andrew cuts them.
- When trimming for time, cut connective filler first and keep the specifics.
- Show Andrew the digest with the script, marking what's used and what's cut.

## Tell the story, don't log the source
- The script must narrate what happened, not describe the forum thread or webpage it came from.
- Bad: "Eight posts, two hours forty-three. His last thread was archived. Replies guess..."
- Good: who the person is, what they claimed, how they say it works, what happened, the creepy turn, the real-world context, then an open ending.
- Arc: Hook -> Claim -> How it supposedly works -> Evidence/what happened -> Creepy turn -> Context/history -> Open, unverified ending.
- Source mechanics (post counts, timestamps, board names, archiving) only if they matter to the story, and in one short line at most.
- Every sentence must say something concrete that can be shown on screen. No filler and no AI-sounding phrasing ("delve", "chilling tapestry", "but here's the thing").
- Every claim is attributed ("he says", "one reply claims"). Say plainly when it can't be verified.

## Tone
- Serious topics: informational, fact-heavy, neutral explanation rather than debunking.
- Lighter topics: funny is fine.

## Safety
- No names or handles of private people. No links to harmful sources, in the video or the description.
- Gore only as a nod (a candle going out, not a body).
- Don't state unconfirmed origins as fact.

## Self-check before showing Andrew
0. Does every line carry a specific detail from the source digest, not a generic summary?
1. Read it aloud as a viewer who never saw the source. Do you know what happened and why it's creepy?
2. Can every line be shown as its own literal world? If not, rewrite the line.
3. Is it 150-170 words, with a hook in the first sentence and no cliffhanger?
4. List every fact you cut so Andrew can ask for any back.
