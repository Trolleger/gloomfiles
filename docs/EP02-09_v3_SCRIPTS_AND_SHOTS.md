# Eps 2-9 v3: scripts on the original mromidev formula + shot lists (for Andrew's OK)

Written 2026-11-26. Replaces EP02-09_v2. Each script follows the original skill's formula line by line, modelled on its example script ("The Bridge That Fell for a Song"): hook, setting, 3-4 escalating beats, snap, a deadpan "Luckily... / Unluckily..." pair, a "Wait...?" beat, "You see," into an everyday "Ever...?" analogy, consequence, and a callback twist that ends on the last word. Jokes are real facts from the sources. The gloom-files rules sit on top. Facts come from shorts/digests/ (Cicada was re-sourced from NPR and the Sunday Telegraph, because Wikipedia leaves out the puzzle steps). Nothing is built.

**Length:** 225-260 words each. That is 61-70 s at the skill's 3.7 words a second, or 78-91 s at Joe's current pace. This needs the same Joe speed-up test as ep 1.

**Shared shot rules:** kit models and our prop library; a new place every ~4 s; push-ins on every hold, kicks on the marked word, hard cuts only on big jumps; Gloom small and off to the side in every shot (acting, never popping in); no spoken cast lines (one narrator, per the original skill); Moth and Biscuit only as silent visual gags; big captions with the key word in colour; in-scene text only for things the script names; lighting goes night-blue for the "You see" explanation, per the style bible. Music: Kevin MacLeod, CC BY, titles checked on incompetech's list.

## EP 2: The Wow! Signal (funny)
Colour: violet space, cyan signal, amber printout; night-blue from "You see". Music: "Comfortable Mystery", then "Lightless Dawn" from shot 10.

### Script (247 words, ~67 s at 3.7 w/s)
The best evidence for aliens we have is six characters circled on a printout, next to the word Wow. It's August 1977, and Ohio State's Big Ear telescope is running the longest alien search in history. Big Ear can't move, so Earth's spin drags the sky past, giving every spot exactly seventy-two seconds. Days later, volunteer Jerry Ehman is checking the printouts by hand when he hits 6EQUJ5. It's not a code, it's loudness, and that U means about thirty times louder than the background. It rises and falls over the full seventy-two seconds, right near the hydrogen line, the channel scientists guessed aliens would use. So Ehman, holding maybe the first message from another world, writes one word. Luckily, Big Ear had two horns, so the second should hear it three minutes later. Unluckily, Big Ear had two horns, and the second hears nothing. Wait, so where did it go? People search in 1987, 1995, 1999 and 2022, and nothing. "We should have seen it again," Ehman says, "when we looked for it fifty times." You see, a real beacon should repeat. Ever heard your name in a crowd, turned around, and nobody's there? A one-off is almost impossible to pin down, so Ehman wondered about an Earth signal bouncing off space junk. In 2012, someone replied anyway, beaming back ten thousand tweets, at a different part of the sky, until the transmitter overheated. Which is why Ehman refuses to draw vast conclusions from half-vast data.

## EP 6: The Dancing Plague of 1518 (informational)
Colour: candle amber and woodcut brown, deep red at dusk; night-blue from "You see". Music: "Village Consort", then "Echoes of Time" from shot 8.

### Script (234 words, ~63 s at 3.7 w/s)

By August, chroniclers count somewhere between fifty and four hundred, drenched in sweat, feet swollen, crying for help. So the council asks its doctors, who say the cure is obvious: dance it out. Some accounts say the city even hired musicians. Luckily, the dancers now had a band. Unluckily, the dancers now had a band. People fear Saint Vitus, said to punish sinners by making them dance, so more join in. Wait, did people actually die? Later accounts say up to fifteen a day, but city records from the time don't mention a single death. Finally, the council bans music and sends the dancers to Saint Vitus' shrine, in red shoes with crosses painted on the soles. You see, historian John Waller thinks it was stress: famine, disease, and a fear of Vitus so strong, bodies acted it out. Ever seen someone yawn, and caught yourself yawning too? Now add hunger, and a curse the whole city believes in. By September, it stops. So the cure wasn't more dancing. It was a new pair of shoes.
