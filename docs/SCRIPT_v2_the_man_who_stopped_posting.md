# Episode 0 (pilot): The Man Who Stopped Posting (v2, ~48 s of voice, awaiting approval)

Series idea (working title: "Gloom Files"): Professor Gloom narrates a creepy internet or history mystery in a deadpan voice. Moth reacts. Biscuit the cat sits somewhere in the background of every episode.

Framing: an anonymous forum poster. No names, no blog URL, no targets, and no claim that any of it is true.

## Voice-over (Professor Gloom, deadpan, ~170 words)
HOOK: A stranger online said he could kill people with his mind. Then he logged off. Which is rude.

SETTING: October. A forum for people with powers. Mostly it's people with Wi-Fi. One of them starts a blog.

ACTION: He logs his experiments. Eight tries. Three "successes." He never says what a success is. He never says how. He just keeps writing the word "failure," like it was never supposed to happen.

SNAP: Then, silence. One week. His thread gets archived. Even the internet buried him.

DEADPAN: Luckily, nobody can prove he killed anyone. Unluckily, nobody can prove he didn't.

EXPLANATION: You see, three out of eight sounds like dark magic, until you remember we only count the hits. It's like wishing a red light turns green. Lights turn green all day. Nobody counts the wishes that did nothing.

CONSEQUENCE: So the forum keeps asking. If he was real, where did he go? And if he was fake, why does everyone keep checking his blog?

CALLBACK: Last week, someone left him a comment. "Didn't think there were others like me."
(beat)
He hasn't replied. Sleep well.

## Look, sound and captions
- Night-time forum room, dark blue and violet, glowing monitors, flashlight under the chin. Warm and bright for the setup, deep night blue from "You see". Biscuit appears in the background.
- Captions: big, bold, 2 to 4 words at a time, timed to the voice. White text with a thick black outline, and the key word in toxic green or blood red. (This overrides the skill's "no captions" rule.)
- Voice: free offline TTS (Piper), one steady deadpan voice.
- Music: an original synthesized minor-key score from the skill's own music scripts, so no licensing at all. Sound effects are synthesized too.
