# AI-label rules for Gloom Files (checked 2026-11-25)

Read from each platform's official help pages. Our setup: cartoon animation, a text-to-speech narrator, no realistic people, music is background only.

## Bottom line
- **Visuals:** no label required on any of the three, because they're clearly cartoons.
- **Voice:** the only open question. Rules turn on "realistic". Use a clearly synthetic-sounding Piper voice (never a clone of a real person) and label anyway on TikTok and Instagram, because it costs nothing.
- **Do not use AI music as the main focus** (YouTube requires disclosure for that). Our background music is synthesized or royalty-free, so that doesn't apply.

## YouTube
- Required for realistic AI content: a real person made to say or do something they didn't, altered footage of a real event, or a realistic scene that didn't happen.
- Exempt: "non-realistic content that's made with AI" and scripts, titles, captions, thumbnails and voice repair. Fully animated content is the page's own example of non-realistic.
- The page does not mention generic text-to-speech narration. It is not on the must-disclose list.
- Where: YouTube Studio, upload flow, "Attributes", "AI use". Answer No for our videos.
- Disclosing does not hurt reach or earning. Consistent failure to disclose when required can lead to labels, removal or loss of Partner Program access.
- YouTube may auto-label content with C2PA metadata or detected AI.
- Source: support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491

## TikTok
- Required: label AI content with realistic images, audio or video. Encouraged: label content completely or significantly AI-generated.
- Cartoons count as AI-generated content in TikTok's definition, but the mandatory part is about realistic content. A clearly synthetic voice is arguably outside it. The pages don't address text-to-speech directly.
- Where: Post screen, "More options", "AI-generated content" switch.
- Labelling doesn't affect distribution if you don't violate guidelines. Falsely labelling unaltered content is a Terms violation. Our content really is AI-made, so no risk.
- Auto-labels (from C2PA metadata or TikTok AI effects) can't be removed afterwards.
- Recommendation: switch it on. It's allowed, free, and avoids any later argument.
- Sources: support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content; tiktok.com/transparency (supporting responsible AI content)

## Instagram and Reels
- Required: photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio, including "a reel narrated with a realistic AI-generated voiceover". Not required: a landscape "in a style resembling a cartoon".
- A natural-sounding TTS voice could count as realistic. A clearly robotic one isn't clearly covered. This is my reading, since the pages don't define "realistic".
- Where: when creating a reel, tap next to "Label as made with AI". The exact control name is unconfirmed.
- Penalties for missing a required label are unspecified. Meta may add "Made with AI" itself if it detects signals.
- There's also an optional "AI creator" profile label for accounts that mostly post AI content. Reach limits for unlabelled accounts weren't in the pages I read, so I can't confirm them.
- Recommendation: use the label on Reels.
- Sources: meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690; help.instagram.com/761121959519495

## What this means for posting
1. YouTube: AI use = No.
2. TikTok: AI-generated content switch = on.
3. Instagram: "Label as made with AI" = on.
4. Keep the narrator voice obviously synthetic. Never clone a real voice.
5. Re-check these pages every few months, since platforms change them.
