# Episode 0 (pilot): "What the Numbers Say" (v4, educational, serious tone, awaiting approval)

Format: an investigation of one public /x/ thread, 8 posts over about 2 h 43 min. Straight debunk. No jokes about deaths. No names, handles, usernames or links. Nothing that helps anyone find the blog or any person. No images copied. No slurs. Nothing about the child-related cases. Post 6 (the gore joke) is not mentioned at all.

Series cast: Professor Gloom narrates, serious and calm, flashlight in hand. No Moth reaction gags and no cat gag in this episode. The cat can return in lighter episodes.
World: a dark "thread room". Posts are paper cards pinned to a corkboard with a time stamp (+0, +33 min and so on). Anons are faceless silhouettes at glowing monitors. Palette is cold blue, with no comedy.
Captions: big, bold, 2 to 4 words at a time, key word in white-on-red for emphasis.
On screen at the start and at the end: "Anonymous online claims. No names. No links. Not evidence that anyone was harmed."

## Voice-over (~245 words, ~66 s)
**HOOK**
A man online claimed he could harm people from a distance. Then he stopped posting. An anonymous board spent three hours trying to explain why. Here's what the numbers show.

**POST 1 · +0 min**
The original post: his last thread was archived. A week of silence, on the board and on his blog.

**POST 2 · +33 min**
The replies offer theories. Real life. No results. Boredom. None of it is evidence. They are guesses about a stranger.

**POST 3 · +1 h**
One reply gives the headline number: three successes out of eight experiments in October. Massive, says the poster, like hitting a one-metre target a kilometre away in a storm.

**ANALYSIS · our analysis, not the thread's**
Here is the problem. The thread never says what counts as a success, or who the targets were. Without a definition made in advance, almost any outcome can be called a hit. Bad things happen to people every day. Pick enough targets, wait long enough, and some will have bad luck. That is selection bias: the hits get counted, the misses disappear.

**POST 4 · +1 h 27**
Another reply notices he kept talking about failure, as if he expected a perfect score. And the rifle comparison fails: a rifleman's shot can be checked. Here, nobody can check the shot, only the story told afterwards.

**POSTS 5, 7, 8 · +2 h 15 to +2 h 43**
Later, one anon says they hadn't thought there were others like them. Another notes the blog takes anonymous comments. A third says they left one.

**CLOSE**
A claim with no clear test, target or record proves nothing. A coincidence is not credit. And real deaths belong to real families. If something like this is weighing on you, talk to someone you trust.
