# Episode pitches (batch 1)

All are well-documented, about events or objects (no private people), so they are safe to cover. Facts must still be verified from sources before scripting.

1. **The Wow! Signal (1977).** A radio telescope logged a 72-second burst from space, and the astronomer circled it and wrote "Wow!". It has never repeated. Risk: low. Good funny or informational fit.
2. **Numbers Stations.** Shortwave radio voices reading out strings of numbers, on air since the Cold War and still running. Risk: low. Intelligence agencies rarely comment, so keep it to documented facts.
3. **Cicada 3301.** An internet puzzle that appeared on 4chan in 2012 and led to real-world posters and cryptography. Creator never confirmed. Risk: low. Many fake "Cicada" groups exist, so show no links.
4. **Max Headroom broadcast hijack (Chicago, 1987).** Two TV stations were hijacked by a masked figure, and nobody was ever identified. Risk: low. Plays to the animation style.
5. **The Dancing Plague of 1518.** Dozens, possibly hundreds, danced in Strasbourg for days, and some reportedly died. Risk: low to medium. Keep deaths factual, and note that estimates vary.
6. **The Voynich Manuscript.** A 15th-century book in an unknown script, never deciphered. Risk: very low. Good for a calm informational episode.
7. **Polybius.** The arcade game said to have made players ill, almost certainly an urban legend. Risk: low. A good funny episode, and it shows how legends spread.
8. **The Bloop (1997).** An ultra-low-frequency ocean sound, later attributed to an icequake. Risk: very low. Good "mostly explained" fit for the series name.

Also considered and held back: Dyatlov Pass and the Lead Masks case (real deaths, identifiable people), and the Somerton Man (a real person's identity and family). Possible later with care.
