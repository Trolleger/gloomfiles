# Ep 5 source digest: Max Headroom signal hijackings
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Headroom_signal_hijackings (read 2026-11-26, full).
1. Sunday, November 22, 1987, Chicago. Two stations hit about two hours apart by at least two unidentified people.
2. 9:14 p.m., WGN-TV, sports segment of The Nine O'Clock News (Chicago Bears report). Screen black about 15 s.
3. Figure in a Max Headroom mask and sunglasses, rocking; behind him a semi-rotating corrugated metal panel imitating Max's background.
4. No audio, only a buzz.
5. WGN engineers changed the studio-to-transmitter link frequency to the tower atop the John Hancock Center. (Length: ~17 to 30 s, sources differ)
6. Anchor Dan Roan: "Well, if you're wondering what's happened, so am I"; joked the news computer "took off and went wild"; restarted his Bears report.
7. About 11:20 p.m., WTTW (PBS), during Doctor Who, "Horror of Fang Rock". This time distorted audio.
8. No engineers on duty at WTTW's tower.
9. Clean, usable bits of what he did: called people "nerds"; held up a can of Pepsi mocking the Coke "Catch the wave" slogan the real Max advertised; sang "Your love is fading"; hummed the Clutch Cargo theme; shouted "I still see the X!"; put on a knitted glove, said his brother "had the other one"; claimed "a giant masterpiece for all the Greatest World Newspaper nerds" (WGN = World's Greatest Newspaper).
10. Crude bits (never in the video): a crude prop, feigned toilet humour, a crude jump cut, a spanking with a flyswatter by a masked woman in a maid costume.
11. "Oh no, they're coming to get me! Ah, make it stop!" while the mask is held up.
12. Faded to static; Doctor Who resumed after about 90 s. WTTW: "attempted to take corrective measures, but couldn't"; "By the time our people began looking into what was going on, it was over". Pirates ended it themselves.
13. Method: a stronger microwave signal into the towers ("capture effect"); needed skill and real transmitter power; likely sent from a spot in line of sight of both towers on two downtown skyscrapers.
14. Nearly impossible after the 2009 switch to digital TV.
15. FCC criminal investigation found no one. Penalty: up to $100,000 fine and a year in prison. Statute of limitations expired 1992.
16. Nobody ever claimed it. Theories: disgruntled insider; Chicago hacker scene.
17. Chicago Tribune 1987: prankster "could become Max Jailroom".
