# Ep 12 source digest: The Buzzer (UVB-76)
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UVB-76 (read 2026-11-26, full; page has sourcing tags).
1. 4625 kHz shortwave, "the Buzzer"; operated by the Russian armed forces; sister stations "The Pip" and "The Squeaky Wheel".
2. Earliest preserved recording 1982 (one cite: on air from 1976).
3. A buzz about 1.2 s long, ~25 a minute, 24 hours a day. Before 2010 a continuous tone the minute before each hour.
4. Name UVB-76 is a mistranscription of UZB-76. Callsigns since: MDZhB, ZhUOZ, ANVF, NZhTI (2020-).
5. First known voice broadcast 1997. Live Russian voice: callsign twice, numbers and a code word, e.g. "NZhTI NZhTI 34 511 GOLOSOK 80 17 81 54".
6. The buzzer seems to sit next to an open mic: 2001 recording caught a voice in the room talking about a "generator".
7. Transmitter: near Povarovo outside Moscow (1997); moved near St Petersburg (2010); since 2015 apparently Naro-Fominsk.
8. Sept 2010: bits of Swan Lake played in place of the buzz.
9. 2011: urban explorers claimed an abandoned base at Povarovo with a radio log for 4625 kHz (weak source).
10. Pirates: 2022 memes broadcast on it; May 4, 2024, a pirate asked questions while the buzz was off and the operator answered in buzzes, "one for yes, two for no"; Dec 2025 hijacks with Swan Lake and pop.
11. Nov 11, 2025: 24 messages, 30 words, "most verbose broadcast in its history" (citation needed).
12. Not the Dead Hand (sometimes called "Doomsday Radio"), page says; the signal breaks down too often.
13. Best-supported theory: a military command network channel; it keeps the frequency occupied and operators alert.
