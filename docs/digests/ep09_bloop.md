# Ep 9 source digest: The Bloop
Sources: oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/bloop.html; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloop (read 2026-11-26).
1. 1997: NOAA researchers listening for underwater volcanoes in the southern Pacific recorded a loud, strange sound.
2. Ultra-low frequency, high amplitude; rose in frequency over about a minute.
3. Hydrophones more than 3,219 km (2,000 miles) apart both caught it (NOAA). [Wikipedia: up to 4,800 km / over 5,000 km; disputed]
4. Recorded many times; unlike anything they'd heard.
5. Array: Equatorial Pacific autonomous hydrophone array (NOAA PMEL), added to the US Navy's SOSUS, a network first built to hunt Soviet submarines.
6. Roughly triangulated to 50°S 100°W, far west of South America's tip.
7. Theories NOAA lists: secret military exercises, ship engines, fishing boat winches, giant squid, whales, an unknown creature.
8. New Scientist 2002 ("Calls from the deep"): a creature "bigger than any whale"; NOAA's Christopher Fox: like an animal's voice but "far more powerful than the calls made by any animal on Earth".
9. Fox told CNN in 2001 he suspected ice calving; said in 2002 it wasn't man-made (submarine, bomb).
10. A 2017 book argues the article "amplified Fox's 'hunch'" into monster talk.
11. NOAA kept moving hydrophones closer to Antarctica; in 2005 found the source on Antarctica's "lonely southernmost land mass": an icequake, an iceberg cracking and breaking off a glacier.
12. Iceberg A53a breaking up near South Georgia (early 2008) produced icequakes with Bloop-like spectrograms.
13. Likely source area: Bransfield Strait to Ross Sea, or Cape Adare (hedged).
14. Seawater carries sound so well that ice noise travels thousands of km.
15. NOAA: about 95% of the ocean unexplored, which fed the theories.
16. No documented fiction link (Lovecraft etc. not in sources). Name origin not given.
