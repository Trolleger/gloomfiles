# Ep 6 source digest: Dancing plague of 1518
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_plague_of_1518 (read 2026-11-26; it relays Waller, Pennant-Rea, Miller).
1. Strasbourg, July to September 1518.
2. First dancer: "Frau Troffea" (4 of 6 chronicles name her; 2 don't).
3. Her daughter is also named as dancing.
4. Started July 14 on the narrow cobbled street outside her half-timbered home (Pennant-Rea).
5. No music; she simply "began to dance".
6. Her husband began mimicking her; within days more than thirty were dancing.
7. She danced for a week, stopping only to sleep, then started again.
8. Three dozen joined soon after; by August 50 to 400 (uncertain).
9. Symptoms as described: convulsions, drenched in sweat, thrashing arms, vacant eyes, feet so swollen they "bleed into their shoes", crying out for help.
10. DISPUTED: up to 15 deaths a day; city records from the time don't mention deaths; claim traced to later accounts.
11. Council handed it to physicians, who prescribed dancing it out ("dance themselves free of it").
12. CLAIM (weakest sourcing): guild halls refurbished, musicians AND strong men hired to hold dancers upright.
13. Backfired: people feared Saint Vitus' punishment and joined to be "free of sin".
14. Council banned public dancing and music.
15. Dancers sent to the shrine of Saint Vitus, believed to punish sinners by making them dance.
16. They wore red shoes sprinkled with holy water, crosses painted on tops and soles, small crosses in hand, incense, Latin incantations.
17. Word spread they had been "forgiven by Vitus".
18. Ended in September; remaining dancers led to a mountaintop chapel to pray.
19. Physician notes, cathedral sermons, chronicles and council notes confirm the dancing (no physician named).
20. Contemporary explanations: demons, divine judgment, "overheated blood" (cure: dance, then rest to cool it).
21. Theories: stress-induced mass psychogenic illness (John Waller; famine, disease, superstition); ergot from rye (rejected by Waller in The Lancet: poisoned people couldn't dance for days, and outbreaks followed rivers with different crops).
22. Precedents: Cölbigk 11th century; tarantism in Apulia.
