# Ep 16 source digest: Oak Island Money Pit
Sources: W = en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Island (thin on the pit); H = history.com/articles/money-pit-oak-island; T = thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/oak-island. Read 2026-11-26.
1. Small private island in Mahone Bay, Nova Scotia, joined to the mainland by a causeway (built 1965 for heavy machinery) (W).
2. 1795, by island lore: 16-year-old Daniel McGinnis finds a depression near a huge oak, with signs a block and tackle had hung there; digs with two friends (T; H says "a local teenager ... with friends").
3. Wooden (oak log) platforms every 10 feet (H, T, W).
4. 1804: a later dig reaches about 90 ft / 30 m and the shaft floods with salt water (T; depths differ between sources).
5. The "90-foot stone" (H): reported in a mid-1800s letter, 2-3 ft long, dark like granite; one reading "Forty feet below two million pounds are buried"; others read it as French/Templar; not seen since the early 1900s.
6. Flood tunnel: a claimed 500-ft tunnel from Smith's Cove refills the pit (H); a dam to stop it was destroyed in a storm (T). Coconut fibre found at Smith's Cove (W, H).
7. Stories documented from 1856, published 1857 (W).
8. 1965: four treasure hunters died in an accident (T).
9. 1960s digging caused collapses around the original pit (H).
10. 1971: a camera lowered to 60 m showed faint images "thought to be" three chests, a pick and a human hand (T, attributed).
11. Theories: Captain Kidd, Henry Morgan, Blackbeard, Inca refugees (T); Templars, Shakespeare codes, Holy Grail, Ark of the Covenant (H); Marie Antoinette's jewels, manuscripts (W).
12. HISTORY's "The Curse of Oak Island" since 2014 (H). Finds: coins (some 16th century), gold links, old china, a lead cross; "more historical than monetary value" (H). T (2015): "no treasure has been found".
13. 50+ books written about it (W).
