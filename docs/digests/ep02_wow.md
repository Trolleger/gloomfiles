# Ep 2 source digest: Wow! signal
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wow!_signal (read 2026-11-26, full).
1. Aug 15, 1977, 22:16 EST, Ohio State's Big Ear telescope (Delaware, Ohio), running the longest SETI program in history.
2. Big Ear could only watch a spot for 72 seconds as Earth turned; the signal lasted the full 72 s.
3. Volunteer Jerry Ehman checked stacks of IBM 1130 line-printer paper by hand; spotted it days later.
4. He circled "6EQUJ5" and wrote "Wow!" beside it.
5. Not a message: intensity levels; digits 1-9, letters from 10 up; peak "U" = about 30x background noise.
6. One reading per 10 s; it rose for 36 s, peaked, fell: exactly what a fixed point in the sky should do.
7. Very narrow, in a single channel of fifty.
8. Near 1420 MHz, the hydrogen line, proposed in 1959 (Cocconi and Morrison) as the channel aliens might use. Exact value disputed (1420.3556 vs 1420.4556; a typo on an oscillator order is the claimed reason).
9. Big Ear had two horns; it appeared in only one, and nobody knows which, so the spot is one of two patches in Sagittarius, near Chi Sagittarii.
10. It should have shown in the second horn 3 minutes later. It didn't.
11. Ehman searched for months. Later searches: 1987, 1989 (Gray), 1995 (Green Bank), 1995-96 (VLA), 1999 (Tasmania, six 14-hour runs), 2022 (Breakthrough Listen, Green Bank + Allen array): nothing.
12. Ehman 1994: "We should have seen it again when we looked for it 50 times." He suspected an Earth signal bouncing off space debris, then doubted it.
13. Ehman 1997: won't draw "vast conclusions from half-vast data".
14. Ehman 2019: "certainly has the potential of being the first signal from extraterrestrial intelligence."
15. 2012: Arecibo beamed back a reply built from about 10,000 tweets; the transmitter overheated early, and none of the targets is in the Wow! region.
16. 2017 comet theory (hydrogen around two comets) rejected: comets weren't in the beam.
17. 2022 paper: one Sun-like candidate star about 1,800 light-years away (candidate only).
18. 2024 preprint (Arecibo data): a rare flare when a star's radiation heated a cold hydrogen cloud. Not peer reviewed.
19. Used in 3 Body Problem (2024), The X-Files (1994), a 2026 Muse album title.
