# Ep 8 source digest: Polybius
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polybius_(urban_legend) (body, read 2026-11-26).
1. Legend: 1981, suburban Portland arcades; a plain black cabinet; abstract, "highly addictive"; players remember the gameplay differently.
2. Claimed effects: seizures, amnesia, insomnia, night terrors, hallucinations.
3. Kids queued and fought over it, playing while getting sicker.
4. Men in black visited, collected data, and LEFT THE COINS INSIDE.
5. It could switch itself on.
6. Gone after "just a few weeks". Some tellings tie it to the CIA's MKUltra.
7. Publisher name in the legend: Sinneslöschen, broken German for roughly "sense delete". No record of it.
8. Name echo: the Greek historian Polybius said only report what you can verify.
9. No 1980s article, no ROM, no real cabinet, only mock-ups.
10. Earliest mention: a coin-op site page dated 1998; the 2000 archive copy has no date and reads "anyone heard of this game?" A 2009 update promised more "after a trip to Kyiv". Nothing ever came.
11. First print: GamePro, September 2003, which called its existence "inconclusive"; the writer only heard of it from that site's owner.
12. 2006: someone claiming to be the founder posted on that site's forum (unverified).
13. Inverse (2020): "almost certainly invented" to promote the site.
14. Real 1981 Portland: two players fell ill at one arcade on the same day: one teen got a migraine after Tempest and blacked out in a stranger's yard; another quit an Asteroids record attempt after 28 hours with stomach pain ("Tummy Derails Asteroids Champ").
15. Ten days later the FBI raided Portland arcades; agents had watched cabinets and recorded high scores to find witnesses; target: machines converted for illegal gambling; 52 arrests in 1981.
16. Atari really did secretly put unfinished test games in arcades and watch players.
17. Later: a 2007 freeware Polybius; Jeff Minter's 2016 VR Polybius (he claimed he'd played the original, then admitted it was part of the hoax); cameos in The Simpsons, Loki.
18. Ripley's: "the most dangerous video game to never exist". Historian: "What H.P. Lovecraft's Necronomicon is for books, Polybius is for videogames."
Safety: legend's "some killed themselves" ending: leave out.
