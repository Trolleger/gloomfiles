# Ep 13 source digest: The Tully Monster
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tully_monster (read 2026-11-26, full).
1. Amateur collector Francis Tully found the first one in 1955 at Mazon Creek, Illinois; the Field Museum couldn't place it in any phylum.
2. ~310 million years old, shallow tropical estuary; up to 35 cm long.
3. Named Tullimonstrum gregarium (1966); "gregarium" = common, there are thousands.
4. Body: cigar-shaped, triangular tail fin, eyes on a long sideways bar, a long proboscis ending in a mouth with up to eight tiny teeth per "jaw". No hard parts.
5. 1989: Illinois state fossil; it's painted on U-Haul trucks.
6. 2016, two Nature papers: its eyes (melanin, two melanosome types) and other features say vertebrate, maybe lamprey-like.
7. 2017: "The 'Tully Monster' is not a vertebrate" (Palaeontology).
8. 2019: squid and cuttlefish also have two melanosome types; eyes alone can't decide.
9. 2020/2022: chemistry of its tissues points to chordate.
10. 2023: 3D scans of 153 specimens: not a vertebrate after all (maybe a non-vertebrate chordate or a protostome).
11. A 1966-68 hoax: a paleontologist invented "dancing worms of Turkana" in letters to the man who named the Tully monster; an expedition was nearly planned.
