# Ep 10 source digest: Kryptos
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kryptos (read 2026-11-26; body verbatim, items 15+ partly from a summary of the tail, marked [S]).
1. Sculptor Jim Sanborn; dedicated Nov 3, 1990, on CIA grounds, Langley, Virginia; cost $250,000 (1988).
2. Copper S-shaped screen like paper coming out of a printer; plus water, wood, plants, red and green granite, white quartz, petrified wood; ~12 x 20 ft.
3. Left side 869 characters; right side a Vigenère tableau. Four sections K1-K4; keyword KRYPTOS.
4. K1 (key PALIMPSEST): "Between subtle shading and the absence of light lies the nuance of iqlusion" (misspelling deliberate in the art).
5. K2 (key ABSCISSA): "...who knows the exact location? Only WW..." with coordinates near the CIA; ends "layer two".
6. K3: paraphrase of Howard Carter opening Tutankhamun's tomb, 1922, ending with Carnarvon's question ("Can you see anything?").
7. Morse code on copper slabs: "virtually invisible", "SOS".
8. 2006: Sanborn revealed a missing S in K2; real ending "west x layer two", confirmed April 19, 2006.
9. Solved K1-K3: an NSA team late 1992 (kept quiet); a CIA analyst by hand 1998; a computer scientist announced it publicly 1999.
10. Sanborn's K4 clues: 2010 NYPVTT = BERLIN; 2014 MZFPK = CLOCK (2025: the World Clock, Alexanderplatz); 2020 NORTHEAST; 2020 EAST.
11. Aug 2025: Sanborn, turning 80, announced he'd auction the K4 solution ("I no longer have the physical, mental or financial resources"); confirmed a K5 exists.
12. Auction Oct 16-Nov 20, 2025; the Kryptos archive sold for $962,500.
13. [S] Sept 2025: two writers found K4 plaintext scraps in Sanborn's papers at the Smithsonian's Archives of American Art; Sanborn confirmed; files sealed until 2075. They refused NDAs.
14. Pop culture: references on The Da Vinci Code dust jacket; Alias.
