# Ep 14 source digest: Sailing stones, Racetrack Playa
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_stones (read 2026-11-26, full).
1. Racetrack Playa, Death Valley; dry, flat lakebed; rocks with tracks behind them.
2. Tracks up to 100 m+ long, 8-30 cm wide; moving stones 15-46 cm, some as heavy as a person.
3. First documented visitor 1915 (a prospector). 1948 first report: wind on mud, no measurements.
4. 1955: heaviest stones too heavy for wind alone; ice sheets proposed.
5. 1968-75: geologists label 30 stones with names (Mary Ann, Nancy, Karen). Nancy, 6.4 cm, moved 260 m total. Karen, ~320 kg, track 170 m, vanished, found again 1996.
6. Winter gusts up to 140 km/h. Time-lapse estimate (2009): moves once every ~3 years, lasting ~10 s (wrong as it turned out).
7. Aug 27, 2014 (Norris et al., PLOS ONE): GPS stones + time-lapse caught 60+ rocks moving on Dec 20, 2013; up to 224 m over that winter. Thin ice panels, a few mm thick, broke on sunny mornings and light wind pushed them at up to 5 m a minute; moves up to 16 min.
8. 2013: rocks stolen (LA Times). 2016: 10 miles of illegal tyre tracks; initials carved in a rock. 2018: volunteers used 750 gallons of water to remove tyre tracks.
