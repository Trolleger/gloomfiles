# Ep 17 source digest: The Hum
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hum (read 2026-11-26, full body).
1. A low hum heard by some, not all; often like a diesel engine idling far off; most never traced.
2. 1973 UK study of 50 complainants: peaks 30-40 Hz, cool weather, light breeze, early mornings.
3. Taos, New Mexico, early 1990s study: at least 2% could hear it, each at a different pitch (32-80 Hz).
4. Middle-aged people more likely to hear it; men and women equally.
5. Sausalito, 1986: traced to midshipman fish mating calls through houseboat hulls.
6. Kokomo, Indiana: a 36 Hz cooling tower and a 10 Hz compressor found and fixed; reports continued.
7. West Seattle: a vacuum pump; reports stopped after silencers were replaced.
8. Windsor, Ontario: 35 Hz from late 2011; 22,000 reports one evening in 2012; linked to Zug Island steelworks; stopped after the blast furnaces shut in April 2020.
9. Auckland 2006: recorded, ~56 Hz.
10. 2009 audiologist estimate: a third physical, two-thirds people over-focusing on ordinary sounds (one researcher's estimate).
11. Spontaneous otoacoustic emissions: 38-60% of normal-hearing adults have ears that make their own faint sounds.
12. One man spent $30,000 investigating; he linked sites to gas pipelines (his view).
13. Recent reports: Germany 2021-22 (faulty AC units, heat pump); Whitehorse, Yukon, Nov 2025.
Skip: reported suicides.
